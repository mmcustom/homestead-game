# Journal Know-how Entries: Text (draft 2026-10-27)

Written by Claude (docs) for Claude Code to use verbatim in Economy/KnowHowRules.cs. Plain player-facing wording. Keys written as the game shows them ("hold E" where the game asks for a hold). If a number or key in the code differs from the text, tell Mike instead of changing the meaning.

The Journal screen gets a seventh section, Know-how. Each entry unlocks the first time its trigger happens (see the plan: first item held, first vessel hung, first site placed).

---

## 1. Ash: Collect and Sell

Trigger: first Ash in the pack.

Burning wood in a campfire or hearth leaves Ash behind. At the fire, press G to collect it (this works whether the fire is lit or out). Firewood leaves 1 Ash for every 3 burned, and Sticks, Branches and Logs leave some too.

Ash sells at the Trading Post for about $2 each, and the price drops a little with every one you sell, then recovers over the next days. You can also leach Ash into Lye, which is worth much more as Potash (see "From Ash to Potash").

## 2. Kindling Bundles

Trigger: first Kindling Bundle held.

Felling trees leaves you plenty of Sticks. In the Craft tab, 10 Sticks make 1 Kindling Bundle, which sells for about $1. Use the amount control to craft several at once.

Sticks also burn and leave a little Ash (1 Ash for every 12 Sticks).

## 3. The Tripod and Boiling Water

Trigger: first Tripod held, or a Cooking Pot first hung.

Boiling needs the Cooking Pot hung on a tripod over a lit fire. Carrying the Pot is not enough.

1. Craft a Tripod (Craft tab, Fire and Light: 3 Branches and 1 Cordage).
2. Open Build, then Other, and click Set Up. Aim at a campfire or hearth within 3 m and click when the ghost is green. The Grill cannot take a tripod.
3. Aim at the tripod and hold E to hang the Cooking Pot from your pack.
4. Stand within 3 m of the lit fire, open Inventory (I) and press Boil on the water.

One vessel hangs at a time, and holding E swaps or takes it down. To dismantle the tripod, put the fire out, equip the Hammer and hold R; the Tripod and the Pot come back to you.

## 4. Tapping Maples

Trigger: first Spile or Sap Bucket held.

Maples run sap only in Winter and Spring, on days that thaw after a freezing night.

1. Craft a Spile (3 Sticks) and a Sap Bucket (1 Log, 3 Branches and 1 Cordage) for each tap.
2. Equip the Spile. While you hold it, a green droplet floats over each Maple within 80 m that you can tap.
3. Walk up and click the Maple. One tap per tree, up to 12.

Your taps show a pail marker, visible from far away and on the minimap: grey while filling, amber when 4 L or more is ready, brown-red when the sap has soured. Hold E at a tap to collect, and R to take the tap down. Sap sours after 48 hours in the bucket, so check your taps about once a day.

## 5. Sap to Syrup

Trigger: first Maple Sap held.

10 L of sap boils down to 1 Maple Syrup in about 12 seconds. You need the Cooking Pot hung on a tripod over a lit fire, and you must stand within 3 m of it. Open Inventory (I), find the Maple Sap row and press Boil. It uses no extra Firewood.

Carried sap does not spoil. Syrup sells for about $10 at the Trading Post, and you can eat it too.

## 6. Using the Leach Hopper

Trigger: Leach Hopper site first placed.

A Leach Hopper turns Ash into Lye. Build it from the Build tab: place the site, deliver 3 Logs and 4 Tall Grass by hand with R (the site holds them between trips), then equip the Hammer and hold click for about 20 blows.

To use it, put the Hammer away. Press R at the hopper to store the Ash and water you carry; you can load Ash alone or water alone, and top up either one later. It holds 12 Ash (with any Lye waiting) and 12 L of water, and the poorest water goes in first. Press E to start a batch: it turns as much Ash as there is water into Lye, 1 Ash and 1 L per Lye, and anything left over waits for the next batch. A batch leaches for 24 game hours, and sleeping counts. Ash and water you add while a batch runs wait for the next one. E collects the Lye when it is ready. With the Hammer equipped, R dismantles the hopper instead and gives back half the materials, with any Ash or Lye inside (the stored water is lost).

## 7. From Ash to Potash

Trigger: first Ash, Lye or Potash Kettle held, or a Leach Hopper site first placed.

Potash sells for far more than the Ash it comes from: about $5 of Potash per Ash, against $2 for the raw Ash. The chain has three steps.

1. Leach. Build a Leach Hopper (see "Using the Leach Hopper"), load Ash and water, and wait 24 game hours.
2. Collect. Hold E at the hopper to take the Lye. Lye also sells by itself for about $3, but it is worth more as Potash.
3. Boil. Buy a Potash Kettle at the Trading Post ($25). Set up a tripod over a lit fire and hold E at it to hang the Kettle. Open Inventory (I) and press Boil on the Lye row: 3 Lye and 2 Firewood make 1 Potash in 18 seconds. The Firewood is used up and leaves no Ash.

Sell the Potash at the Trading Post (about $16 each).

## 8. Charcoal: Burning Logs Slowly

Trigger: first Charcoal held, or a Charcoal Pit site first placed.

Charcoal is Logs burned slowly under earth. It burns far hotter than Firewood and is the fuel iron smelting needs in a Bloomery (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery"). You make it in a Charcoal Pit.

1. Open Build, then Other, and place a Charcoal Pit Site on open dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and trees, and not under a roof.
2. Deliver 4 Clay and 6 Tall Grass with R over as many trips as you like. Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build the pit.
3. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the pit to load the Logs you carry (up to 6 in all; a Log weighs 8 kg, so a full pit may take two loads).
4. Press E to light it once it holds 3 Logs. It burns for 24 game hours, even while you sleep or are away, and the Logs are used up. 3 or 4 Logs make 2 Charcoal, 5 make 3 and 6 make 4.
5. Press E again to collect the Charcoal.

Charcoal sells at the Trading Post for about $1. With the Hammer equipped, R takes the pit down, but not while it burns; you get back half the Clay and Tall Grass and all the Logs and Charcoal inside.

## 9. Iron Ore at the Trading Post

Trigger: first Iron Ore held.

There is no iron to dig on your land yet, so iron ore is bought. The Trading Post sells Iron Ore for $4 each (3 kg). It buys it back for only $1, so buy only what you will use.

Smelting iron needs Charcoal as well (see "Charcoal: Burning Logs Slowly") and a furnace, the Bloomery (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery").

## 10. Smelting Iron in the Bloomery

Trigger: first Bloomery site placed, or first Iron Bar held.

Iron ore is smelted in a Bloomery, a small furnace of Stone and Clay that burns Charcoal.

1. Buy Iron Ore at the Trading Post and make Charcoal in a Charcoal Pit (see those two entries).
2. Open Build, then Other, and place a Bloomery Site on open dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and trees, and not under a roof.
3. Deliver 8 Stone and 6 Clay with R over as many trips as you like (about 61 kg in all, so a Hand Cart helps). Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build the bloomery.
4. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the bloomery to load the Ore and Charcoal you carry, up to 4 of each. You can load one without the other and top up either later.
5. Press E to start a smelt once it holds 2 Ore and 2 Charcoal. Each 2 Ore and 2 Charcoal make 1 Iron Bar, up to 2 Bars at a time, and the smelt takes 10 game hours, even while you sleep or are away. Ore and Charcoal you add while it smelts wait for the next smelt.
6. Press E again to collect the Iron Bars.

Nobody buys Iron Bars; forge them into iron goods at a Smithy (see "The Smithy: Forging Iron Goods"). With the Hammer equipped, R takes the bloomery down, but not while it smelts; you get back half the Stone and Clay and all the Ore, Charcoal and Bars inside.

## 11. The Smithy: Forging Iron Goods

Trigger: first Smithy site placed, or first Iron Bar held.

A Smithy turns Iron Bars and Charcoal into iron goods that sell at the Trading Post, and into an iron Shovel and Pick Axe for your own use. Its forge and anvil stand under a roof.

1. Make Iron Bars first (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery").
2. Open Build, then Other, and place a Smithy Site on dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and, for the forge fire, 3 m clear of trees. Turn it with the mouse wheel so the open front faces where you want to work.
3. Deliver 6 Stone, 3 Clay, 2 Logs, 4 Branches and 6 Tall Grass with R over as many trips as you like (about 61 kg in all, so a Hand Cart helps). Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build it.
4. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the Smithy to store the Iron Bars, Charcoal, Sticks and Cordage you carry (it holds 8 Iron Bars, 8 Charcoal, 6 Sticks and 4 Cordage).
5. Press E to open the Smithy screen and choose what to forge. Door Hinges take 3 game hours; a Hook Set and a Box of Nails take 2. Each uses 1 Iron Bar and 1 Charcoal. An Iron Shovel (2 Iron Bars, 2 Charcoal, 3 Sticks and 1 Cordage) and an Iron Pick Axe (2 Iron Bars, 2 Charcoal, 2 Sticks and 1 Cordage) take 3 game hours each. A job runs even while you sleep or are away.
6. Press E at the Smithy to collect the finished item. One job runs at a time, and the next cannot start until the item is collected.

Door Hinges sell for about $26, a Hook Set for about $18 and a Box of Nails for about $16, and each price drops a little with every one you sell, then recovers over the next days. The iron Shovel and Pick Axe cannot be sold. They work like the stone ones but need a quarter fewer blows for everything: a Stone from the outcrop, a stump, Clay and Sand, a Well shaft and a new Outhouse pit. The stone tools stay as they are. With the Hammer equipped, R takes the Smithy down, but not while something is being forged; you get back half the materials and all the Bars, Charcoal, Sticks, Cordage and finished items inside.

## 12. Fighting with your hands and a Knife

Trigger: first punch thrown, or first Knife held.

Click with empty hands to punch. Fists only stun and startle: a small animal (a Rabbit, Squirrel, Turkey or Duck) stops for about a second and then bolts, a Deer just bolts, and nothing is ever hurt.

Equip a Knife and click to slash, then click again to stab. The clicks alternate on their own, and the first click after a short pause is always a slash. Both reach about 1.5 m. A Knife kills small game (a Rabbit, Squirrel, Turkey or Duck) in one solid hit and leaves a Carcass to field dress, but a Deer is only startled, never hurt.

A punch costs 2 Stamina and a slash or stab 3. Every blow makes noise: animals within 8 m of a punch and 6 m of a Knife blow bolt, so you will rarely catch one that has already noticed you. A blow cannot reach through a wall. A trapped animal cannot be struck; take it out of the trap with a Knife in your pack (it does not have to be equipped).
