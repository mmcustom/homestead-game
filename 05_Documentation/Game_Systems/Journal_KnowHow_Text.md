# Journal Know-how Entries: Text (draft 2026-10-27)

Written by Claude (docs) for Claude Code to use verbatim in Economy/KnowHowRules.cs. Plain player-facing wording. Keys written as the game shows them ("hold E" where the game asks for a hold). If a number or key in the code differs from the text, tell Mike instead of changing the meaning.

The Journal screen gets a seventh section, Know-how. Each entry unlocks the first time its trigger happens (see the plan: first item held, first vessel hung, first site placed).

---

## 1. Ash: Collect and Sell

Trigger: first Ash in the pack.

Burning wood in a campfire or hearth leaves Ash behind. At the fire, press G to collect it (this works whether the fire is lit or out). Firewood leaves 1 Ash for every 3 burned, and Sticks, Branches and Logs leave some too.

Ash sells at the Trading Post for about $2 each, and the price drops a little with every one you sell, then recovers over the next days. You can also leach Ash into Lye, which is worth much more as Potash (see "From Ash to Potash").

## 2. Kindling Bundles

Trigger: first Kindling Bundle held.

Felling trees leaves you plenty of Sticks. In the Craft tab, 10 Sticks make 1 Kindling Bundle, which sells for about $1. Use the amount control to craft several at once.

Sticks also burn and leave a little Ash (1 Ash for every 12 Sticks).

## 3. The Tripod and Boiling Water

Trigger: first Tripod held, or a Cooking Pot first hung.

Boiling needs the Cooking Pot hung on a tripod over a lit fire. Carrying the Pot is not enough.

1. Craft a Tripod (Craft tab, Fire and Light: 3 Branches and 1 Cordage).
2. Open Build, then Other, and click Set Up. Aim at a campfire or hearth within 3 m and click when the ghost is green. The Grill cannot take a tripod.
3. Aim at the tripod and hold E to hang the Cooking Pot from your pack.
4. Stand within 3 m of the lit fire, open Inventory (I) and press Boil on the water.

One vessel hangs at a time, and holding E swaps or takes it down. To dismantle the tripod, put the fire out, equip the Hammer and hold R; the Tripod and the Pot come back to you.

## 4. Tapping Maples

Trigger: first Spile or Sap Bucket held.

Maples run sap only in Winter and Spring, on days that thaw after a freezing night.

1. Craft a Spile (3 Sticks) and a Sap Bucket (1 Log, 3 Branches and 1 Cordage) for each tap.
2. Equip the Spile. While you hold it, a green droplet floats over each Maple within 80 m that you can tap.
3. Walk up and click the Maple. One tap per tree, up to 12.

Your taps show a pail marker, visible from far away and on the minimap: grey while filling, amber when 4 L or more is ready, brown-red when the sap has soured. Hold E at a tap to collect, and R to take the tap down. Sap sours after 48 hours in the bucket, so check your taps about once a day.

## 5. Sap to Syrup

Trigger: first Maple Sap held.

10 L of sap boils down to 1 Maple Syrup in about 12 seconds. You need the Cooking Pot hung on a tripod over a lit fire, and you must stand within 3 m of it. Open Inventory (I), find the Maple Sap row and press Boil. It uses no extra Firewood.

Carried sap does not spoil. Syrup sells for about $10 at the Trading Post, and you can eat it too.

## 6. Using the Leach Hopper

Trigger: Leach Hopper site first placed.

A Leach Hopper turns Ash into Lye. Build it from the Build tab: place the site, deliver 3 Logs and 4 Tall Grass by hand with R (the site holds them between trips), then equip the Hammer and hold click for about 20 blows.

To use it, put the Hammer away. Press R at the hopper to store the Ash and water you carry; you can load Ash alone or water alone, and top up either one later. It holds 12 Ash (with any Lye waiting) and 12 L of water, and the poorest water goes in first. Press E to start a batch: it turns as much Ash as there is water into Lye, 1 Ash and 1 L per Lye, and anything left over waits for the next batch. A batch leaches for 24 game hours, and sleeping counts. Ash and water you add while a batch runs wait for the next one. E collects the Lye when it is ready. With the Hammer equipped, R dismantles the hopper instead and gives back half the materials, with any Ash or Lye inside (the stored water is lost).

## 7. From Ash to Potash

Trigger: first Ash, Lye or Potash Kettle held, or a Leach Hopper site first placed.

Potash sells for far more than the Ash it comes from: about $5 of Potash per Ash, against $2 for the raw Ash. The chain has three steps.

1. Leach. Build a Leach Hopper (see "Using the Leach Hopper"), load Ash and water, and wait 24 game hours.
2. Collect. Hold E at the hopper to take the Lye. Lye also sells by itself for about $3, but it is worth more as Potash.
3. Boil. Buy a Potash Kettle at the Trading Post ($25). Set up a tripod over a lit fire and hold E at it to hang the Kettle. Open Inventory (I) and press Boil on the Lye row: 3 Lye and 2 Firewood make 1 Potash in 18 seconds. The Firewood is used up and leaves no Ash.

Sell the Potash at the Trading Post (about $16 each).

## 8. Charcoal: Burning Logs Slowly

Trigger: first Charcoal held, or a Charcoal Pit site first placed.

Charcoal is Logs burned slowly under earth. It burns far hotter than Firewood and is the fuel iron smelting needs in a Bloomery (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery"). You make it in a Charcoal Pit.

1. Open Build, then Other, and place a Charcoal Pit Site on open dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and trees, and not under a roof.
2. Deliver 4 Clay and 6 Tall Grass with R over as many trips as you like. Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build the pit.
3. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the pit to load the Logs you carry (up to 6 in all; a Log weighs 8 kg, so a full pit may take two loads).
4. Press E to light it once it holds 3 Logs. It burns for 24 game hours, even while you sleep or are away, and the Logs are used up. 3 or 4 Logs make 2 Charcoal, 5 make 3 and 6 make 4.
5. Press E again to collect the Charcoal.

Charcoal sells at the Trading Post for about $1. With the Hammer equipped, R takes the pit down, but not while it burns; you get back half the Clay and Tall Grass and all the Logs and Charcoal inside.

## 9. Iron Ore at the Trading Post

Trigger: first Iron Ore held.

There is no iron to dig on your land yet, so iron ore is bought. The Trading Post sells Iron Ore for $4 each (3 kg). It buys it back for only $1, so buy only what you will use.

Smelting iron needs Charcoal as well (see "Charcoal: Burning Logs Slowly") and a furnace, the Bloomery (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery").

## 10. Smelting Iron in the Bloomery

Trigger: first Bloomery site placed, or first Iron Bar held.

Iron ore is smelted in a Bloomery, a small furnace of Stone and Clay that burns Charcoal.

1. Buy Iron Ore at the Trading Post and make Charcoal in a Charcoal Pit (see those two entries).
2. Open Build, then Other, and place a Bloomery Site on open dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and trees, and not under a roof.
3. Deliver 8 Stone and 6 Clay with R over as many trips as you like (about 61 kg in all, so a Hand Cart helps). Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build the bloomery.
4. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the bloomery to load the Ore and Charcoal you carry, up to 4 of each. You can load one without the other and top up either later.
5. Press E to start a smelt once it holds 2 Ore and 2 Charcoal. Each 2 Ore and 2 Charcoal make 1 Iron Bar, up to 2 Bars at a time, and the smelt takes 10 game hours, even while you sleep or are away. Ore and Charcoal you add while it smelts wait for the next smelt.
6. Press E again to collect the Iron Bars.

Nobody buys Iron Bars; forge them into iron goods at a Smithy (see "The Smithy: Forging Iron Goods"). With the Hammer equipped, R takes the bloomery down, but not while it smelts; you get back half the Stone and Clay and all the Ore, Charcoal and Bars inside.

## 11. The Smithy: Forging Iron Goods

Trigger: first Smithy site placed, or first Iron Bar held.

A Smithy turns Iron Bars and Charcoal into iron goods that sell at the Trading Post, and into an iron Shovel and Pick Axe for your own use. Its forge and anvil stand under a roof.

1. Make Iron Bars first (see "Smelting Iron in the Bloomery").
2. Open Build, then Other, and place a Smithy Site on dry ground, 3 m clear of other buildings and, for the forge fire, 3 m clear of trees. Turn it with the mouse wheel so the open front faces where you want to work.
3. Deliver 6 Stone, 3 Clay, 2 Logs, 4 Branches and 6 Tall Grass with R over as many trips as you like (about 61 kg in all, so a Hand Cart helps). Then equip the Hammer and hold click on the site to build it.
4. Put the Hammer away. Press R at the Smithy to store the Iron Bars, Charcoal, Sticks and Cordage you carry (it holds 8 Iron Bars, 8 Charcoal, 6 Sticks and 4 Cordage).
5. Press E to open the Smithy screen and choose what to forge. Door Hinges take 3 game hours; a Hook Set and a Box of Nails take 2. Each uses 1 Iron Bar and 1 Charcoal. An Iron Shovel (2 Iron Bars, 2 Charcoal, 3 Sticks and 1 Cordage) and an Iron Pick Axe (2 Iron Bars, 2 Charcoal, 2 Sticks and 1 Cordage) take 3 game hours each. A job runs even while you sleep or are away.
6. Press E at the Smithy to collect the finished item. One job runs at a time, and the next cannot start until the item is collected.

Door Hinges sell for about $26, a Hook Set for about $18 and a Box of Nails for about $16, and each price drops a little with every one you sell, then recovers over the next days. The Trading Post also stocks the iron Shovel ($12) and Pick Axe ($15). They work like the stone ones but need a quarter fewer blows for everything: a Stone from the outcrop, a stump, Clay and Sand, a Well shaft and a new Outhouse pit. The stone tools stay as they are. With the Hammer equipped, R takes the Smithy down, but not while something is being forged; you get back half the materials and all the Bars, Charcoal, Sticks, Cordage and finished items inside.

## 12. Fighting with your hands and a Knife

Trigger: first punch thrown, or first Knife held.

Click with empty hands to punch. Fists only stun and startle: a small animal (a Rabbit, Squirrel, Turkey or Duck) stops for about a second and then bolts, a Deer just bolts, and nothing is ever hurt.

Equip a Knife and click to slash, then click again to stab. The clicks alternate on their own, and the first click after a short pause is always a slash. Both reach about 1.5 m. A Knife kills small game (a Rabbit, Squirrel, Turkey or Duck) in one solid hit and leaves a Carcass to field dress, but a Deer is only startled, never hurt.

A punch costs 2 Stamina and a slash or stab 3. Every blow makes noise: animals within 8 m of a punch and 6 m of a Knife blow bolt, so you will rarely catch one that has already noticed you. A blow cannot reach through a wall. A trapped animal cannot be struck; take it out of the trap with a Knife in your pack (it does not have to be equipped).

## 13. Food Spoils: Rot and Compost

Trigger: first Rot held.

Fresh food does not keep. The Inventory and every storage screen show how long each food has left: green while fresh, amber once it is past half its life, red when it is about to spoil. Heat spoils food faster, cold slows it, and freezing weather nearly stops it. A Food Cache keeps food a little longer than your pack.

Eating food that is amber or red can make you sick, and the closer it is to spoiling, the more likely.

Food that spoils turns into Rot. Build a Compost Bin (Build tab: 6 Sticks and 2 Cordage, with the Hammer), then press R at it to put in Rot and Tall Grass. Every 4 Rot and 2 Tall Grass become 1 Fertilizer in about 7 days, slower in the cold and not at all while frozen. Press E to take the Fertilizer out. The Trading Post buys it until you have crops to use it on.

## 14. Drying Food on a Rack

Trigger: first dried food held.

A Drying Rack turns fresh food into dried food that is light to carry and keeps for months. Build it from the Build tab: 4 Sticks, 2 Branches and 2 Cordage, with the Hammer.

Press R at the rack to hang Blackberries, Raspberries, Wild Apples, Pawpaws, mushrooms, raw meat or raw fish, up to 12 pieces at a time. They dry only in daylight while it is not raining or snowing: fruit and mushrooms need about 2 days, meat and fish about 3. Food on the rack still ages while it dries, but at half the usual rate.

Fruit and mushrooms dry one for one. Meat and fish are cut into strips: the more a piece is worth cooked, the more strips, from 4 for a Small Game Meat or a Bluegill to 8 for a Venison. Dried Meat and Dried Fish are safe to eat without cooking. Press E to take the dried food off; the Trading Post pays more for it than for fresh or cooked food.

---

## 15. Salting Meat and Fish

Trigger: first Salt held.

Salt keeps raw meat and fish for half a year. Buy Salt at the Trading Post ($2 a bag) and carry a Crock Pot (shaped at the Potter's Shed and fired in the Kiln).

Away from a fire, open Inventory (I): raw meat and fish rows show a Salt button. Each piece takes 1 Salt for every 2 kg it weighs (a Venison takes 2) and is cut into Salt Meat or Salt Fish straight away, one for every dollar it is worth cooked: 4 from a Venison, 2 from a Bluegill. The crock is not used up.

Salt Meat and Salt Fish keep for 180 days and are eaten as they are, no cooking needed. The Trading Post pays more for them than for the same meat fresh or cooked, even after the Salt.

---

## 16. Smoking Meat and Fish

Trigger: first Smoked Meat or Smoked Fish held, or a Smokehouse Site marked out.

A Smokehouse turns raw meat and fish into Smoked Meat and Smoked Fish, the best-paying food the Trading Post buys. Build it from the Build tab: mark out the site, deliver 10 Stone and 4 Clay for the fire pit, then 12 Logs and 6 Lumber, and finish it with the Hammer.

Put the Hammer away and press R at the smokehouse to load raw meat and fish (up to 20 pieces) and Firewood (up to 12), then press E to light the fire. A load smokes for a day and burns 6 Firewood. While the fire burns, the meat does not age. If the Firewood runs out, the smoking stops and the meat starts to age again until you load more Firewood.

Each piece gives one Smoked Meat or Smoked Fish for every dollar it is worth cooked: 4 from a Venison, 2 from a Bluegill. Smoked food keeps for 180 days and is eaten as it is. Press E to collect it; the Trading Post pays $3 a piece, more than for salted, dried or cooked meat.

## 17. The Root Cellar

Trigger: a Root Cellar Site marked out.

A Root Cellar is an earth-covered room for keeping food. Food on its shelves ages a third as fast as outdoors, in every season, and it never freezes: raw meat keeps about 10 days, apples about 6 weeks, and smoked, dried and salted food far longer.

Build it from the Build tab. Mark out the site, dig it out with the Shovel, then deliver 10 Stone, 8 Logs and 4 Lumber in any order and finish it with the Hammer. Pick dry ground out of hollows, or rain will run in.

Walk in through the door and press E at the shelves at the back to store or take food. The shelves hold up to 300 kg of food.

## 18. Sawing Lumber in a Saw Pit

Trigger: a Saw Pit Site marked out.

A Saw Pit turns Logs into Lumber, so you need not buy it at the Trading Post. Build it from the Build tab: mark out the site, dig the trench with the Shovel, then deliver 6 Logs and 4 Branches in any order and finish it with the Hammer.

Put the Hammer away and press R at the pit to lay Logs across it (up to 6). Then equip the Wood Saw and hold click on the log. Every 2 Logs take 20 strokes and make 3 Lumber, stacked beside the pit. Press E to take the Lumber.

## 19. Hides Spoil: Salt or Tan Them

Trigger: a Deer Hide, Small Furs, a Salted Deer Hide or Salted Furs in the pack.

A fresh Deer Hide or Small Furs will not keep. A Deer Hide spoils in about a week and Small Furs in about five days, faster in summer heat and slower in the cold, and then they turn to Rot. The Inventory shows how long each has left, as it does for food.

To keep a hide, salt it. Buy Salt at the Trading Post ($2 a bag), then open Inventory (I): a raw hide row shows a Salt button. A Deer Hide takes 2 Salt and Small Furs take 1. A salted hide keeps for about a year and sells for the same as a fresh one.

---

## 20. The Hide Frame: Scraping and Rawhide

Trigger: building a Hide Frame, or Rawhide or Dried Furs in the pack.

A Hide Frame is a square pole frame for working hides, built in one go with the Hammer: 4 Sticks, 2 Branches and 2 Cordage. Find it under Other in the build list.

Lace a hide into it with R: a Deer Hide, or up to 4 Small Furs, raw or salted. Then equip the Knife and hold click on the hide to scrape it clean: 20 strokes for a Deer Hide, 8 for furs. Scraping takes off the flesh and fat, and the hair from a Deer Hide; furs keep their hair.

The scraped hide then dries on the frame in daylight while it is not raining, about 12 hours. A dried Deer Hide is Rawhide, stiff and lasting, which the Trading Post buys for $12. Dried Furs fetch $4 each. Press E to take them off the frame.

---

## 21. Brain Tanning: Buckskin and Furs

Trigger: a Soft Hide, Soft Furs, Buckskin or Tanned Furs in the pack.

Brain tanning makes a hide soft and lasting. Lace Rawhide or Dried Furs into the Hide Frame with R. With a Crock Pot and 1 litre of water in your pack (a Canteen or Bucket), equip the Knife and hold click on the hide to work the brain-and-water mix into it: 30 strokes for a hide, 10 for furs. Each animal's brains are enough for its own hide.

The worked hide is a Soft Hide or Soft Furs, white and floppy. Take it off with E and smoke it in the Smokehouse with the meat (R loads it). A Soft Hide takes the room of 2 pieces of meat and Soft Furs 1, and it smokes for the same day on the same Firewood.

It comes out as Buckskin, golden brown and soft, or Tanned Furs. The Trading Post buys Buckskin for $24 and Tanned Furs for $6 each.

---

## 22. Bark Tanning: Leather

Trigger: building a Tanning Vat, or Tanbark or Leather in the pack.

Bark tanning makes Leather, the strongest hide of all, but it takes a week. You need Rawhide from the Hide Frame, Tanbark and water.

To get Tanbark you need a Bark Spud, a short iron blade for peeling bark: forge one at the Smithy (1 Iron Bar, 1 Charcoal and a Stick) or buy one at the Trading Post. With it in your pack, the Inventory's Logs row has a Strip button (unless an axe is in your hand). Each Log gives 2 Tanbark and becomes a Peeled Log, which still works anywhere a Log does.

Build a Tanning Vat (3 Logs and 2 Clay, one click with the Hammer, under Other). Load it with R: Rawhide, up to 4, with 4 Tanbark and 20 litres of water for each hide. Press E to start the soak. After 7 days the hides are Leather, which the Trading Post buys for $30 each. E takes it out.

## 23. The Sawmill

Trigger: marking out a Sawmill site.

A Sawmill saws Logs into Lumber on its own, with a water wheel turned by the creek. It is the next step after the Saw Pit: you need Lumber from the pit to build it.

Build it from the Build tab, under Other. Mark the site out on the creek bank, within 4 m of the water but out of the creek itself; the wheel goes on the creek side. Deliver 12 Logs, 10 Lumber, 2 Iron Bars, 4 Cordage and 6 Stone with R, in any order, then hammer it up.

Load Logs with R, up to 20. Every hour the wheel saws 2 Logs into 3 Lumber, even while you are away or asleep, and E takes the Lumber. It stops during a flash flood and starts again by itself when the creek goes down.

## 24. The Kitchen Garden

Trigger: marking out a Garden Bed, or first carrying the Hoe.

A kitchen garden feeds you and earns money. Mark out a Garden Bed from the Build tab, under Other: each bed is a 4 by 4 ft square, and beds can stand side by side. Dig each one with the Shovel (hold click).

Buy seed at the Trading Post: seed packets, tomato plants, and potatoes for planting. With the Hoe in hand, aim at a dug bed and press E to plant; R picks which seed. Crops grow day by day with the warmth: lettuce in about 50 days, tomatoes 75, potatoes 100. Tender crops (corn, beans, tomatoes, squash) die in any frost, so plant them after the last spring frost, around the middle of May; lettuce, carrots, potatoes and kale can go in from April.

Rain waters the beds. In a dry spell, fill a Watering Can or Bucket and press E on a bed to water it. When a crop is ready, E harvests it; tomatoes, beans and kale keep giving pickings for weeks. A Shovel's R fills an empty bed back in.

## 25. Better Beds: Soil, Raised Beds and Frost

Trigger: first Raised Bed Site placed, or first Row Cover or Fertilizer held.

Every crop taken to its end wears a bed's soil. A rich bed grows about 3 crops before it is spent, and a spent bed gives three quarters of a full harvest; beans put a little back. The bed's prompt shows its soil: rich, good, worn or spent. When there is nothing else to do at a bed, E spreads one Fertilizer, and 4 bring a spent bed back to rich. Make Fertilizer in a Compost Bin, or buy it at the Trading Post ($1.25).

A Raised Bed is warmer and drains. Place a Raised Bed Site from the Build tab (under Other), deliver 2 Lumber with R, hammer the frame up, then fill it with the Shovel. Or hold the Hammer at a dug bed and press R with 2 Lumber in your pack. Its crops are about a week ahead in spring, and heavy rain never waterlogs it.

A Row Cover ($6 at the Trading Post) keeps 3 C of frost off a crop. R lays it over a bed and R takes it off; it is used again and again. Above 27 C, a crop under it grows a little slower.

At 6 pm, if tonight's low could hurt a crop, a message says how many beds are at risk, and whether a Row Cover will be enough.

## 26. Saving Your Own Seed

Trigger: first garden crop harvested, or first Tomato Seed held.

Seed you save costs nothing. With the Hoe in hand, press R on a growing square before its first pick to mark it saved for seed (R again unmarks it). A saved square is never picked: it stays in the ground until the seed is ripe, then E collects seed for 4 squares, with some food for most crops.

Beans, tomatoes, lettuce, both corns and squash ripen their seed the same summer. Corn needs at least 2 saved squares of the same corn standing together, or it sets no good seed. Potatoes are their own seed: keep some from the harvest. Carrots and kale need a winter in the ground first: after about 6 weeks of cold they grow again, and the seed is ripe the next summer. E with 4 Tall Grass mulches a saved carrot or kale square against hard frost.

Saved Tomato Seed sown straight into a bed starts about a month behind a bought Tomato Plant, unless you start it on a seedling bench in a Glass Greenhouse. Seed keeps 2 to 4 years; old seed comes up poorly, and the Inventory shows its age once it is a year old.

## 27. Fences and Garden Raiders

Trigger: first fence section or gate placed, or first Wire Mesh held.

At dawn and dusk, rabbits and deer come to nibble open garden beds, and over a season a bed can lose about a quarter of its crop. If you are nearby you will see the animal walk in and eat: come close or shoot near it and it bolts, and the crop is spared.

A closed ring of fence keeps them out. Build fences from the Build tab, under Other, with the Hammer:
- Wattle Fence: 4 Sticks and 6 Branches a section, 1 m high. It keeps rabbits out; deer jump it.
- Deer Fence: 1 Log and 1 Wire Mesh ($15 at the Trading Post) a section, 7 ft high. It keeps deer and rabbits out.
- Gate: 2 Lumber and 1 Cordage. It swings open as you walk into it and shuts behind you.

A new section snaps to the end of the fence near where you aim, and one that would close a gap is cut to fit. Only a closed ring protects, and a planted bed says whether it is fenced, and against what. R with the Hammer takes a section down.

## 28. Cold Frames

Trigger: first Cold Frame built, or first Window Sash held.

A Cold Frame is a low box with a glass lid that keeps a few garden squares warm. Build one from the Build tab (under Other) with the Hammer: 2 Window Sashes ($8 each at the Trading Post) and 2 Lumber for each square, in Small (1 square), Medium (2) or Large (3). Its beds come with it, already dug.

Shut, the lid keeps the inside about 5 C warmer at night, and the sun can heat it far more by day. Propped open, it stays close to the outside air. E on the front board props the lid or shuts it, and the frame shows the inside temperature. Shut it on frosty nights and open it on sunny days, or tender greens cook. A Vent Opener does this for you (see "Vent Openers").

Lettuce, kale, carrots and beans fit under the lid. No rain gets in with the lid shut, so water the beds; deer and rabbits cannot reach them. For extra warmth, E with 2 Fertilizer digs a hotbed into a frame's bed: it warms the soil about 8 C for 4 weeks. R with the Hammer takes the frame down once its beds are empty.

## 29. Hoop Houses

Trigger: first Hoop House Site placed, or first Greenhouse Hoop or Greenhouse Plastic held.

A Hoop House is a walk-in tunnel of plastic over steel hoops, with its beds inside. Place a Hoop House Site from the Build tab (under Other) on dry, fairly level ground, then deliver Lumber, Greenhouse Hoops, Greenhouse Plastic and Cordage with R. A Small house (8 squares) takes 8 Lumber, 6 Hoops, 1 Plastic and 2 Cordage; the Medium (16 squares) and Large (36) take more. Hoops are $12 at the Trading Post or forged at a Smithy (1 Iron Bar and 1 Charcoal), and Plastic is $20 a sheet. Then hold click with the Hammer.

With the sides down the house keeps about 5 C of warmth at night and gains a lot in the sun; E at the door rolls the sides up on warm days. No rain falls inside, so water the beds.

Snow piles on the plastic while it snows. With 2 cm or more on it, E at the door sweeps it off, an hour's work. If it reaches 30 cm the plastic tears, and plastic wears out after 4 years anyway. A torn house gives no cover until E fits new sheets from your pack.

---

## 30. Glass Greenhouses and Seedling Benches

Trigger: first Glass Greenhouse Site placed, or first Glass Pane or Start held.

A Glass Greenhouse is the warmest cover, and the glass lasts for good. Place a Glass Greenhouse Site from the Build tab (under Other), deliver Lumber, Stone, Glass Panes ($3 each at the Trading Post) and Boxes of Nails with R, then hold click with the Hammer. A Small one (4 squares and 1 bench) takes 16 Lumber, 10 Stone, 24 Panes and 1 Box of Nails; the Medium (9 squares) and Large (30) take more. No Iron Bars are needed.

With the vents shut it keeps about 7 C of warmth at night and heats up fast in the sun; E at the door opens or shuts the vents. No rain falls inside, so water the beds.

The seedling benches along the back start transplants. With seed in your pack, E sows a tray (R picks which seed). When it is ready, E takes up the transplants: 1 Tomato Seed gives 12 Tomato Plants in 30 days, and 1 Lettuce, Kale or Squash Seed gives 3 Starts. Plant them with the Hoe like seed; they start well grown, and wilt if left unplanted for a week.

## 31. Heating a Greenhouse

Trigger: first stove, heater or barrels built, or first Greenhouse Stove, Propane Heater or Propane Tank held.

Each greenhouse has a heater spot and a barrel spot inside, and the build ghost snaps to them. Build these with the Hammer from the Build tab:
- Wood Stove: 1 Greenhouse Stove ($40 at the Trading Post) and 2 Stone. R loads Firewood (up to 8) and E lights it. It burns one Firewood an hour, only while the house would be below 8 C.
- Propane Heater: 1 Propane Heater ($35). R loads Propane Tanks ($12, up to 2) and E turns it on. It keeps the house at 5 C or above and uses propane only for the warmth it adds.
- Water Barrels: 4 Logs. They soak up the sun by day and give the warmth back at night, with no fuel.

A banner says when a stove or heater runs out. The Small Hoop House has no room for a heater, and the Small Glass Greenhouse none for barrels. R with the Hammer takes them out, with any fuel inside, and a greenhouse with heat in it cannot be taken down until they are out.

## 32. Watering Without the Can

Trigger: first Hose Bib, Hose Reel or pump built, or first Garden Hose, Drip Kit, pump or Gasoline held.

Fittings water many beds at once. Each draws from the nearest Water Barrel, Rain Barrel or pump within 15 m that has water. Build them with the Hammer:
- Hose Bib: 1 Garden Hose ($8 at the Trading Post). It is fitted inside a greenhouse beside the door and waters that greenhouse's beds.
- Hose Reel: 1 Garden Hose and 1 Lumber, outdoors. It waters the open garden beds within 12 m.
- Hand Pump: 1 Hand Pump ($30) and 2 Lumber, within 5 m of the creek or a pond, or beside a Well. E pumps 20 L into its 200 L tank.
- Fuel Pump: 1 Fuel Pump ($90) and 2 Lumber, beside the creek or a pond. R loads Gasoline ($3 a can, up to 4); each can pumps about 400 L.

E at a bib or reel soaks all its beds at once. With a Drip Kit ($15) in your pack, E fits it, and from then on it waters by itself every morning at 6. When it can't, a banner that morning and the fitting's prompt say why.

## 33. Vent Openers

Trigger: first Vent Opener built or held.

A Vent Opener ($35 at the Trading Post) opens and shuts a cover for you, with no power. Its wax swells as it warms and pushes the lid, the sides or the vents open. Build it with the Hammer on the inside of a Cold Frame's back wall, or inside a Hoop House or Glass Greenhouse beside the door; the build ghost snaps to the spot.

Every hour it opens the cover when the inside would reach 24 C (75 F) shut, and shuts it when it would stay at 18 C (64 F) or below. In between it leaves it as it is. You can still open or shut the cover by hand with E, but the opener has its say again the next hour.

E at the opener says what it is doing. R with the Hammer takes it off, and a frame or greenhouse with one fitted cannot be taken down until it is off.

## 34. Deliveries from the Trading Post

Trigger: first Delivery Pad built.

The Trading Post sells only what you can carry, unless it delivers. Build a Delivery Pad first: it is free, from the Build tab (under Other), and you can have one at a time.

At the Trading Post (T), press the button at the top of the store's list to switch from Carry to Deliver. Each store row then adds to the order instead of your pack, with no weight limit. The order on the right shows the goods, the delivery fee and the weight. Place Order pays for it all at once: $50 a delivery, or free when the goods come to $1,000 or more.

The order arrives on the pad at 8 a.m.: the next day for up to 500 kg, in 2 days for up to 2,000 kg, and in 3 days for more. A banner says when it is there. Take what you want with E; the pad keeps the rest, and R puts things back. Take the pad up with the Hammer once it is empty, and build it again wherever you want deliveries.

## 35. The Pole Barn

Trigger: first Pole Barn Site marked out.

A Pole Barn is a big post-frame building for storing everything the homestead needs. It comes in three sizes: Small (24 x 32 ft, holds 5,000 kg), Medium (30 x 40 ft, 10,000 kg) and Large (40 x 60 ft, 20,000 kg). Mark out a Pole Barn Site from the Build tab (under Other); it is free and needs fairly flat, open ground.

Then, at the Trading Post (T), switch to Deliver: your site has two choices. Order the kit ($7,500, $12,000 or $22,000) and it is delivered onto the site: posts, trusses and steel sheets. Build it yourself in three stages with the Hammer: set the posts, raise the trusses, then put on the roof and walls. Or hire the crew ($13,000, $20,000 or $36,000): half is paid now and half when they finish. They start 3 days later and take about a week.

Walk in through the big door and press E at the shelves along the back to store things or take them. It holds anything but water.
