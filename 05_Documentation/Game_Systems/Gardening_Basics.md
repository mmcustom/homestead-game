# Gardening Basics

Design doc, drafted by Claude on 2026-11-25; Mike answered its open questions the same day (section 9). Nothing here is built. This is the outdoor garden that comes before the greenhouses (Greenhouses.md): a greenhouse bed is one of these beds under cover.

Everything marked *proposed* is Claude's suggestion for Mike to change as he likes.

---

## Mike's decisions (2026-11-25)

- **Crops:** 8 staples to start: potatoes, corn, beans, tomatoes, lettuce, carrots, squash and kale (a root, a grain, a legume, a fruit, greens, and a winter-hardy crop).
- **Ground:** dig beds in the dirt with a tool (free), and build raised beds from Lumber (cost more, drain and warm faster).
- **Seeds:** buy seed at the Trading Post, then let some plants go to seed and keep it for next year, so you can stop buying.
- **Pests:** deer and rabbits (animals already in the game) eat unfenced crops; a fence, or a greenhouse, keeps them out.

---

## 1. Where the game's garden is (real-world timing)

The property is in the Bethel, Ohio area. Cincinnati's averages: last spring frost about April 19, first fall frost about October 17, roughly 180 frost-free days (Urban Farmer's Ohio table; Ohio averages about 170). The game's real 365-day calendar and its weather already make frosts, so the garden uses them as they come: a late frost can kill tender seedlings, an early one can end the season.

Cool-season crops stand light frost and can grow on until a hard freeze; a second, fall planting works for them (Ohio State's garden calendar: plant July to September for a fall garden).

## 2. Beds

A **bed square** is 4 ft by 4 ft (1.2 m by 1.2 m), the unit for the garden and the greenhouses.

| Bed | How | Cost (*proposed*) | Pros and cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dug bed | Aim at bare ground and dig with the Shovel (*proposed*: 12 blows), like the Saw Pit's digging. Placed with the ghost, snapping to a grid beside other beds. | Free | Cheap. Wet ground stays wet: slower in a wet spring, can flood in a creek flood if put in the floodplain. |
| Raised bed | Built on the ground from Lumber, one click with the Hammer, then filled with soil (dig it, or Fertilizer counts as soil). | *Proposed*: 2 Lumber per square | Warms earlier in spring (*proposed*: plant a week earlier), drains (no waterlogging), easier to reach. |

Rules (*proposed*): beds need open sky (not under trees), not in a hollow, not in the creek channel. A **Hoe** (new tool, Trading Post or Smithy) keeps the bed weeded and is the planting tool; without one you plant by hand, slower. Weeds are left for later.

## 3. The eight crops (*proposed* numbers from extension tables)

Days to harvest, frost tolerance and yields are from Ohio State, Missouri, Maine, Kansas State, Cornell and the Old Farmer's Almanac (sources at the end). A **square** is one 4 × 4 ft bed square. Yields are for a healthy, watered, fed square; they are converted from the "per 10 ft of row" tables.

| Crop | Plant as | When (Bethel) | Days to harvest | Frost | Per square (*proposed*) | Keeps (spoilage) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | Seed potatoes (bought by the kg, or your own) | Early April | 90 to 110 | Semi-hardy: leaves frost-killed, tubers fine | 9 kg | Months in the Root Cellar |
| Field corn | Seed | Mid May | 85 to 100, then dries on the stalk | Tender | 16 ears; dried, about 2 kg of kernels | Dried corn keeps a year; grinds to cornmeal later, and later livestock feed |
| Sweet corn | Seed | Mid May | 70 to 85 | Tender | 16 ears, eaten fresh | Days (sweetness fades fast); can be dried |
| Beans (bush) | Seed | Mid May, again in July | 55 green, about 90 to dry; the player chooses per square | Tender | 2 kg green, or 0.7 kg dry beans | Green: a week. Dry: a year |
| Tomatoes | Transplants: start seed indoors or in a cold frame 6 to 8 weeks early, or buy plants | Mid May (after last frost) | 70 to 80 from transplanting, picked over weeks | Tender | 4 plants, 14 kg over the season | A week or two |
| Lettuce | Seed | April, again late August | 45 to 60 | Light frost OK; bolts and turns bitter in summer heat | 16 heads (or 2 kg of leaves) | Days |
| Carrots | Seed | April, again early August | 70 | Frost-tolerant, can stay in the ground into winter | 3.5 kg | Months in the Root Cellar |
| Squash (winter) | Seed | Late May | 100 | Tender | 1 plant sprawling a square: about 6 squash, 9 kg | Months, cool and dry |
| Kale | Seed | April, again August | 60, picked for months | Frost-hardy: picks all winter, sweeter after frost | 2.7 kg over the season | A week |

Growth (*proposed*): each crop counts **growing days**, a day counting fully when the day's temperature is in the crop's range, partly when cool, and not at all when cold (or too hot for lettuce). So a cold spring is slow and a warm one fast, and the greenhouse's warmth speeds things up. Each crop shows stages: sprouting, growing, flowering or forming, ready, then going to seed or past its best if left.

## 4. Caring for the garden

- **Water:** rain waters outdoor beds. Each bed has a soil moisture level (*proposed*) that rain fills and sun and heat dry out. In a dry spell or a Heat Wave you water by hand (bucket, Watering Can), later with a hose from a barrel (Greenhouses.md section 6 lists the upgrades, which work outdoors too). A dry bed stresses the crop (slower growth), then cuts the yield, then kills it.
- **Feeding:** each bed has a fertility level (*proposed*: a full bed grows two crops before it runs low). Spreading **Fertilizer** (from the Compost Bin) refills it. **Beans put some back** (legumes fix nitrogen), so rotating beans through a bed saves Fertilizer. A low bed gives a smaller harvest.
- **Frost:** a frost below a crop's tolerance damages or kills it. The Weather screen's forecast warns of frost; a **Row Cover** (Trading Post) laid over a bed protects it by a few degrees, the same cover used inside the greenhouses.
- **Harvest:** use key on a ready square. Some crops are picked once (potatoes, carrots, corn, squash, lettuce heads), some many times over weeks (tomatoes, beans, kale, leaf lettuce).

---

## 5. Seeds and seed saving

- **Buying:** seed packets at the Trading Post, one packet plants one square (*proposed*: $3 a packet; seed potatoes $2 a kg, 2 kg a square; tomato plants $2 each). Seeds are a new item group in the market.
- **Saving your own** (*proposed*): mark a ready square "Save for seed" instead of harvesting it. It is left to ripen fully and dry (beans dry in the pod, tomatoes ripen past eating, lettuce bolts and flowers), then gives back seed for several squares and only a little food. Saved seed keeps 2 to 5 years (*proposed*: as the extension charts give).
- How easy it is follows the real crops:
  - **Easy:** beans, tomatoes and lettuce (they pollinate themselves). **Potatoes:** keep some of the harvest as next year's seed potatoes.
  - **Same year, more plants needed** (*proposed*): corn and squash cross-pollinate (wind and insects), so the game asks for more plants to save from (*proposed*: two squares of corn, one of squash). With only your own garden there is no neighbour's crop to cross with. But **field corn and sweet corn cross with each other** (wind carries corn pollen about half a mile), so corn seed saved while both are flowering comes up mixed (*proposed*: the saved seed is marked "crossed" and gives a poorer crop). Grow one kind for seed that year, or plant them far enough apart in time that they flower weeks apart.
  - **Two years:** carrots and kale flower in their second year, so a square marked for seed must live through the winter (kale does outdoors; carrots need mulch or a cold frame) and gives seed the next summer.

---

## 6. Deer, rabbits and fences

- **Rabbits** nibble young plants and greens (lettuce, kale, beans, carrot tops). **Deer** browse almost everything, especially beans, corn, tomatoes and kale. The animals already wander the property; near an unfenced garden, a nearby one comes to eat at dawn and dusk (*proposed*), taking a share of a square each visit.
- **Fences** (*proposed*), placed as posts with the ghost, with a gate:
  - **Wattle fence** (Sticks and Branches woven between stakes, waist high): keeps rabbits out, not deer.
  - **Deer fence** (posts, 7 to 8 ft, with wire mesh from the Trading Post): keeps both out.
- A greenhouse keeps both out.
- Hunting or trapping the raiders works too: the garden can bring the meat to you.

---

## 7. Selling and eating

All eight crops are food (eaten raw or cooked, using the existing cooking) and sell at the Trading Post. They spoil on the existing spoilage clock; potatoes, carrots and squash keep for months in the Root Cellar; dry beans and dried corn keep about a year; tomatoes and greens must be eaten or sold fast (or dried, using the Drying Rack). Prices (*proposed*) are set when the slice is built, with fresh produce in winter worth more (the greenhouse's payoff).

## 9b. Mike's answers for slice GB2 (2026-11-25)

1. **Fertility:** gentler. A full bed grows 3 crops before it runs low, and an empty bed still gives 75% of a full harvest. Fertilizer (Compost Bin, or $0.50 at the Trading Post) puts it back; beans put a little back.
2. **Raised beds:** staged, and any bed. Mark one out, bring 2 Lumber, hammer it up, then fill it with soil using the Shovel. An existing dug bed can also be upgraded with 2 Lumber and the Hammer. The soil is warmer (about a week earlier in spring) and it drains, so heavy rain can't waterlog it.
3. **Row Cover:** $6 at the Trading Post, laid over a bed with R and taken off again, reusable. It keeps a light frost off (about 3 °C of protection); left on in hot weather it slows the crop a little.
4. **Frost alert:** yes. In the evening, if frost is forecast and tender crops are uncovered, a message says so.

## 9c. Mike's answers for slice GB3 (2026-11-25)

1. **Seed back:** a square left to go to seed gives seed for **4 squares**, plus a little food.
2. **Corn:** no crossing between field corn and sweet corn. Corn just needs **2 squares** of the same kind saved together to set good seed; squash needs 1.
3. **Seed age:** yes, seed ages. Old seed sprouts less often, then not at all.
4. **Order:** the one-year crops first (GB3a). Carrots and kale, which make seed only in their second year, come in GB3b after Mike plays GB3a.

## 10. As built: slice GB1 (2026-11-25)

**Beds.** Garden Bed is in the Build menu (Other). Place a 1.2 m square (at least 1.25 m from another bed, 1.5 m from a tree): it shows as stakes and twine. Dig it with a Shovel (12 blows) to turn the soil. R with a Shovel fills a bed back in (or removes an undug one).

**Planting and care (E, in this order).**
1. With the Bucket or Watering Can (8 L) in hand and water in it: water the bed.
2. With the Hoe in hand: plant the selected seed. R cycles through the seeds you carry.
3. A dead crop: clear it.
4. A ready crop: harvest it ("Harvested {name} x{units}").

**Growing.** Every game hour (GardenBeds.Hour from SpoilageManager.OnHour): growth by warmth, soil moisture (dries in the heat, rises with rain), frost damage by each crop's hardiness, lettuce bolting in the heat. Real days to harvest. Beans are picked green, or left to dry (90 days, 7 units). New beds start at half moisture.

**The Trading Post.** Hoe 10.00, Watering Can 8.00, each seed packet 3.00, Tomato Plant 2.00 (on the shelf only from mid-April to early June, after the last frost: Mike, 2026-11-25), Potatoes (to plant or eat) 0.60 (the store pays 0.25). Garden produce sells in its own group (Produce): up 10% in Spring, down 10% in Fall.

**Planting at the wrong time (2026-11-25).** Planting a frost-tender crop before about early May, or any crop that frost hurts so late that its growing days run past the usual first frost (about mid-October), shows a red warning under the plant line. The garden bed's prompt now shows its status on its own lines, with each key line saying only what that key does.

**Know-how.** "The Kitchen Garden" (entry 24) opens when you first place a bed or carry a Hoe.

**Art.** Hoe, Watering Can, 7 seed packets, 10 produce items and the Tomato Plant with icons; the bed (marked and dug, darker when wet); and 45 growing-plant models, one per crop per stage (sprouting, growing, maturing, ready, dead). Details in Art_Needed_List.md.

**Not in GB1 (next slices).** Fertility and Fertilizer, raised beds and Row Cover (GB2); seed saving (GB3); deer, rabbits and fences (GB4).

## 11. As built: slice GB2 (2026-11-25)

**Soil fertility.** Each bed keeps how worn its soil is. A crop taken to its end (all its picks) wears a third of it, so a rich bed grows 3 crops before it is spent; beans put a tenth back instead. A spent bed gives 75% of a full harvest (the cut grows as the soil wears). The bed's prompt shows the soil: rich, good, worn or spent. **E spreads one Fertilizer** when there is nothing else to do there (no water to pour, nothing to plant or harvest): each one puts back a quarter, so 4 bring a spent bed back to rich. Beds from before GB2 load rich.

**Raised beds.** Build tab > Other > **Raised Bed Site**: mark it out (free), deliver 2 Lumber with R, hammer the frame up (10 blows), then fill it with soil using the Shovel (12 blows, like digging). Or hold the Hammer at a dug Garden Bed and press R with 2 Lumber in your pack. A raised bed's soil is 1.5 °C warmer for growing (about a week ahead in spring), and it drains: soil soaked past 90% slows a dug bed's crop (waterlogged, 60% growth) but never a raised bed's. Taking a raised bed apart with the Shovel gives the 2 Lumber back.

**Row Cover.** $6 at the Trading Post. R lays it over a dug bed and R takes it off again; it is reused, and comes back if the bed is filled in. Under it the crop takes 3 °C less frost (a tender crop survives a -2 °C night but not -4 °C); rain still comes through; above 27 °C the crop under it grows at 80%.

**The frost alert.** At 6 pm, if the night's expected low (the weather model's coldest hour, with today's weather; it can still change) comes within 1.5 °C of hurting a bed's crop, a message names how many beds are at risk, and says so too when even a Row Cover will not be enough.

**Art.** The raised bed's board frame, the Row Cover draped over its hoops on a bed, the folded Row Cover item and the Raised Bed Site's build icon.

## 12. As built: slice GB3a (2026-11-25)

- **Saving a square:** with the Hoe in hand, R on a growing square marks it "saved for seed" (before its first pick). R again unmarks it until the seed is ripe. A saved square is never picked: it stays until the seed is ripe, then E collects it.
- **Seed back:** seed for **4 squares**, less if the crop was damaged (at least 1), plus a little food. Collecting the seed wears the soil like any crop.

| Crop | Seed ripe at (growing days) | Food that comes with it | Seed keeps (years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beans | 90 (left to dry; marking beans also sets them to dry) | 2 Dry Beans | 3 |
| Tomatoes | 95 (ready + 20) | 18 Tomatoes | 4 |
| Lettuce | 85 (bolts and flowers; it isn't hurt or killed by bolting) | none | 3 |
| Field Corn | 110 (ready + 15) | 4 Field Corn | 2 |
| Sweet Corn | 103 (ready + 25) | none | 2 |
| Squash | 120 (ready + 20) | 3 Squash | 4 |

- **Corn:** as each saved corn square reaches its ready day (flowering), at least **2** saved squares of the same corn at half-grown or more must be standing, or it sets no good seed. A banner says so when it happens, and collecting then gives only the food.
- **Potatoes** are their own seed (keep 8 from the harvest). **Carrots and kale** can't be saved yet: they need a second year (GB3b).
- **Tomato Seed** (new item): saved from your own tomatoes. With the Hoe, R picks it after the Tomato Plants. It is sown straight into a bed and starts **30 growing days** behind a bought plant (about 105 growing days in all), since there is no greenhouse yet to start it in.
- **Seed age:** every seed packet (bought or saved) keeps the day it was got. It is good for its full life in years, about half comes up in the year after, then none. Planting uses the oldest seed carried first. The plant prompt warns in red when the seed is old, and the Inventory shows the oldest packet's age once it is a year or more (for example "3 yrs old - half sprout"). Seed that fails is used up, and the bed stays bare.
- **Art:** Tomato Seed packet and icon (item_seed_tomatoes). The plants going to seed reuse the ready look.
