# Homestead
## Wildlife System v1.0

Status: Design Draft

---

# Purpose

The Wildlife System controls animal populations, behavior, habitats, migration patterns, and interactions with the player.

Wildlife is a primary source of:

- Food
- Fur
- Hides
- Discovery
- Knowledge

Wildlife should feel like part of a living ecosystem rather than randomly spawned resources.

---

# Core Philosophy

Animals live on the land whether the player is present or not.

Wildlife should:

- Follow routines
- Use habitat
- Respond to weather
- Respond to season changes
- React to hunting pressure

Knowledge of animals should produce better results than simply exploring randomly.

# Habitat System

Animals prefer specific habitats.

Examples:

Rabbit:

- Brush lines
- Field edges
- Dense cover

Deer:

- Forest edges
- Meadows
- Water corridors

Turkey:

- Forest openings
- Mature timber

Habitat quality affects population density.

---

# Daily Activity Cycles

Animals operate on schedules.

---

## Dawn

High activity:

- Deer
- Turkey

Good hunting opportunity.

---

## Midday

Reduced movement.

Animals feed less aggressively.

---

## Dusk

High activity.

Second major hunting period.

---

## Night

Limited visibility.

Different species become active.

Future expansion may increase night activity systems.

---

# Seasonal Behavior

## Spring

Wildlife populations increase.

Breeding activity begins.

Animal movement increases.

---

## Summer

Abundant food.

Animals disperse.

Water sources become important.

---

## Fall

Heavy feeding activity.

Preparation for winter.

Excellent hunting season.

---

## Winter

Reduced food availability.

Movement patterns change.

Animals concentrate around resources.

---

# Water Dependency

Many animals depend on water.

Examples:

- Deer trails often intersect water sources.
- Waterfowl require wetlands.
- Rabbits prefer availability of nearby moisture.

Water systems and wildlife systems are connected.

Not Alpha 0.1

- Hunting pressure

# Design Rules

1. Wildlife should feel alive.

2. Animal behavior should be learnable.

3. Knowledge should outperform luck.

4. Seasons should influence wildlife.

5. The land should feel like an ecosystem, not a resource generator.
