# Homestead
## Livestock System v1.0

Status: Design Draft

---

# Purpose

The Livestock System bridges the gap between survival and self-sufficiency.

Livestock provide:

- Food
- Milk
- Eggs
- Breeding stock
- Brush removal
- Future income

Livestock represent one of the first major investments in a growing homestead.

---

# Core Philosophy

Animals are not passive resource generators.

They require:

- Water
- Feed
- Shelter
- Time
- Care

Players who neglect animals will experience reduced productivity and potential losses.

---

# Early Livestock Progression

## Goats

Primary Benefits:

- Milk
- Cheese Production
- Brush Removal
- Breeding Stock

Reasons for Early Access:

Goats are versatile and can thrive on rough terrain.

They help convert undeveloped land into productive pasture.

# Animal Needs

All livestock require:

## Water

Clean water must be available daily.

Water needs increase during:

- Summer
- Drought
- High activity

---

## Feed

Animals require ongoing nutrition.

Examples:

Goats:
- Browse
- Hay
- Supplemental feed

Chickens:
- Grain
- Insects
- Kitchen scraps

Rabbits:
- Hay
- Greens

---

# Feed Reserves

Winter preparation includes:

- Hay storage
- Feed storage

Insufficient feed creates:

- Reduced production
- Weight loss
- Animal health issues

---

# Shelter Requirements

Animals require protection.

Examples:

Goats:
- Small barn
- Three-sided shelter

Chickens:
- Coop

Rabbits:
- Hutches

Shelter improves survival and productivity.

---

# Breeding

Livestock populations can grow naturally.

Benefits:

- Herd expansion
- Meat production
- Sale of breeding stock

Breeding should require planning.

Uncontrolled breeding can create feed shortages.

---

# Design Rules

1. Livestock should feel valuable.

2. Animals should require care.

3. Feed reserves matter.

4. Water access matters.

5. Animals support self-sufficiency before profit.

6. Goats remain one of the earliest and most useful livestock choices.
