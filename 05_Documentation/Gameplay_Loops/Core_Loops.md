# Homestead
## Gameplay Loops v1.0

Status: Design Draft

---

# Purpose

This document describes how the systems already defined in Game_Systems and Systems combine into actual play sessions — the day-to-day, week-to-week, and year-to-year experience of playing Homestead.

It does not redefine any system's rules. Every mechanic referenced here is governed by its own doc; this document only shows how they interact in sequence.

---

# Core Philosophy

No single system is fun in isolation. The loop is what's fun — the constant, slightly uncomfortable tension between what needs doing and what there's time for.

Every loop below should leave the player with unfinished business. Per the Time System's design rule, there should always be more work available than can be completed in a day.

---

# The Daily Loop (Year One, Survival Phase)

Wake
↓
Check Hydration, Hunger, Fatigue, Health, Morale
↓
Secure Water (Water System)
↓
Tend Fire (Core Survival System)
↓
Check Traps, if any placed (Trapping System)
↓
Primary Food Activity — Forage, Fish, or Hunt, depending on season and daylight (Foraging / Fishing / Hunting System)
↓
Discovery Opportunities along the way (Discovery System)
↓
Return to Shelter Before Dark
↓
Eat, Preserve Surplus (Core Survival System — Spoilage)
↓
Choose Sleep — Early, Normal, Late, or All-Nighter (Core Survival System — Sleep)

The player rarely completes every step above in a single day. Which steps get skipped, and which get prioritized, is the actual gameplay.

---

# The Weekly Loop — Fall (Preparation Season)

Fall is explicitly the season where "most winter success is determined" (Season System). The weekly rhythm shifts toward stockpiling:

Hunting Trips — primary food activity, dawn and dusk (Hunting System, Wildlife System)
↓
Food Preservation — smoking, drying meat and fish before it spoils (Core Survival System — Spoilage, Hunting System, Fishing System)
↓
Firewood Production — stockpiling fuel ahead of Winter's increased consumption (Core Survival System)
↓
Late Foraging — nuts, apples, pawpaw, the last harvest window of the year (Foraging System)
↓
Building Push — any construction the player wants finished before the ground and water freeze (Building and Housing System)

A player who under-invests in this loop enters Winter under-supplied. Nothing forces this outcome directly — the seasonal availability windows do it naturally.

---

# The Winter Loop — The Annual Survival Test

Winter is explicitly described as the "annual survival test" across Season System, Wildlife System, and Water System. The daily loop above still applies, but nearly every branch narrows:

- Primary food activity shifts from Hunting/Foraging to Trapping (Trapping System, Season System)
- Water sourcing narrows to springs and wells as surface water freezes (Water System)
- Firewood consumption rises, drawing down whatever was stockpiled in Fall (Core Survival System)
- Exposure becomes a live risk if shelter or clothing is inadequate (Health System — Exposure System, Weather System)
- Discovery slows — less new ground gets covered, more time is spent maintaining what's already built

A player who prepared well in Fall experiences Winter as tense but manageable. A player who didn't experiences compounding shortages — the intended consequence, not a punishment bolted on separately.

# The Economic Loop

Personal Use
↓
Stored Reserves
↓
Livestock Needs
↓
Surplus
↓
Market Sales

This is the Economy System's core philosophy, restated here as a loop rather than a priority list. In practice it plays out weekly once Stage 2 livestock arrives: eggs, milk, and surplus produce accumulate, and the player decides — session by session — how much to hold back for winter versus sell for the tools, materials, or breeding stock the Economic Sinks require (Economy System).

Selling too early or too much creates the Winter Pressure the Economy System describes directly. This loop is where that pressure is actually felt by the player.

# The Year-Over-Year Loop

Spring — Exploration, Water Discovery, Foraging Restart
↓
Summer — Food Gathering, Property Development, Peak Fishing
↓
Fall — Hunting, Preservation, Firewood, Preparation
↓
Winter — Survival, Trapping, Planning
↓
(repeat, with more property, more knowledge, more infrastructure)

Per the Discovery System's Knowledge Progression, the property should feel different to the player each year — not because the land changed, but because the player's understanding of it deepened. Year One is about learning the loop above. Year Three and beyond are about running it more efficiently and building the infrastructure (Stage 2+ per the GDD) that eases its tightest constraints.

---

# Design Rules

1. A loop is only worth documenting here if it combines at least two existing systems — a single system's own rhythm belongs in that system's doc, not here.

2. Every loop should end with unfinished business. If a player can comfortably complete everything in a day, the loop needs more pressure, not more content.

3. Consequences for under-preparing should emerge from existing systems interacting (e.g., skipped Fall preservation → Winter food shortage), not from a separate punishment system layered on top.

4. This document describes intended play rhythm for pacing and balancing reference. It is not a tutorial or onboarding script.
